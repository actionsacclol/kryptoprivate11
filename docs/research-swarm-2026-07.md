# Krypt Sniper — Infrastructure & Competitive Research Report (July 2026)

**Executive summary.** The mid-2026 landscape strongly favors Krypt Sniper's positioning: closed terminals all charge ~1% per trade and have suffered trust collapses (Axiom insider front-running, BullX shutdown), while the open-source "sniper bot" niche is saturated with wallet-drainer malware and the only credible OSS competitor is explicitly not production-grade. Technically, the path is clear. Detection: free tiers everywhere remain websocket-only, so the free default stays on native logsSubscribe, with a single Yellowstone gRPC adapter (the universal protobuf API every provider now speaks) as the pluggable paid upgrade — Shyft's $199/mo unmetered gRPC+shreds plan and Chainstack's $49 add-on bracket the paid range, and Jito ShredStream dies September 5, 2026 (successor: DoubleZero Edge). Execution: Helius Sender is free on all plans (0 credits, 0.000005 SOL swqosOnly tips), giving even free-tier users staked-connection landing; paid users escalate to Sender Max/Nozomi/0slot 0.001 SOL tips plus Jito bundles priced from the live tip_floor feed. Protocol: Pump.fun has moved to a unified v2 trade interface (27-account buy_v2, mandatory volume accumulators, dynamic market-cap-tiered fees, USDC pairs since May 21, 2026) with roughly one breaking change per quarter — sometimes unannounced — so account layouts must be treated as volatile config with auto-halt on error spikes. The single biggest strategic asset Krypt Sniper already owns is its replay/shadow-mode pipeline: nothing in the competitive set, free or paid, offers deterministic replay or paper trading.

## 1. RPC & Streaming

The market has stratified into four latency tiers: free JSON-RPC websockets, Yellowstone gRPC/Geyser (commoditized to $49–199/mo via Chainstack/Shyft, gated at $499/mo by Helius/QuickNode), shred-level feeds, and raw shreds via DoubleZero Edge — which replaces Jito ShredStream, shutting down **September 5, 2026** ([Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnfeed/)). Every serious provider (Helius LaserStream, QuickNode, Shyft, Chainstack, Triton, ERPC, Solana Vibe Station) exposes the same Yellowstone protobuf API, so one gRPC adapter is portable across all of them plus self-hosted nodes ([rpcpool/yellowstone-grpc](https://github.com/rpcpool/yellowstone-grpc)). No provider gives free gRPC; all free tiers are HTTP RPC + standard websockets. The latency ordering shreds < gRPC < websockets < HTTP polling is well supported, but all concrete millisecond figures are vendor/blogger claims — no independent methodology-published benchmark exists (unverified magnitudes; Helius cites websockets adding "400ms to 30s" depending on commitment, [Helius shreds post](https://www.helius.dev/blog/solana-shreds)).

**What Krypt Sniper should do:** Keep native logsSubscribe as the free default. Ship exactly one Yellowstone gRPC client (@triton-one/yellowstone-grpc, configurable endpoint + x-token header, from_slot resume) that works across every provider and self-hosted nodes. Default subscriptions must be aggressively filtered (Pump.fun program ID only, exclude failed txs, no block streams) so byte-metered providers (QuickNode, Triton PAYG) stay cheap — a tight Pump-only stream on Triton at $0.08/GB may be the cheapest legit gRPC path. Do **not** build on Jito ShredStream; if a shreds lane ships, target DoubleZero Edge or Shyft RabbitStream (Yellowstone-compatible, no UDP/Reed-Solomon work). Document Shyft Build ($199) as the recommended power-user tier and Chainstack ($49) as the budget gRPC setup. Free users: split rate limits across two free keys (e.g. Helius for WS hot path, Alchemy for HTTP enrichment).

## 2. Transaction Landing

The 2026 standard pattern: percentile-based compute-unit price scoped to your writable accounts, ~0.001 SOL tip to a rotated tip wallet, fan out through parallel lanes (Jito block engine + staked/SWQoS senders), same signature everywhere (runtime dedupes). Nearly every commercial sender (0slot, Nozomi, bloXroute, NextBlock, Helius Sender Max) has converged on a 0.001 SOL minimum tip. Two key 2026 shifts: Helius Sender is **free (0 credits) on all plans** including free tier, and Triton made SWQoS free-by-default (retired Cascade Feb 28, 2026) — basic staked sending is commoditizing toward free, driven by Anza bandwidth improvements.

Context (unverified/vendor-grade): Jito tips reportedly grew to 60%+ of Solana priority-fee volume, with winning searchers surrendering ~50–70% of expected profit on contested plays ([Chainstack analysis](https://chainstack.com/jito-explained-bundles-tips-mev-solana/)); bloXroute's Jan 2025 self-benchmark showed 98–100% land rates across all lanes at 0.001 SOL under calm conditions — 18 months stale and vendor-run, treat as directional only ([benchmark](https://bloxroute.com/pulse/benchmarking-solana-transaction-speeds-and-landing-rates/)).

**What Krypt Sniper should do:** Free tier: Helius Sender swqosOnly (5000-lamport tip) — near-zero cost, no separate vendor integration, upgrades to Sender Max on the same code path. Multi-lane sender state machine: one signed tx fanned to N lanes (signature dedupe makes this safe), maxRetries=0 + skipPreflight, own retry loop keyed on lastValidBlockHeight (~151 blocks / ~60s), rebuild+re-sign with fresh blockhash fetched at processed commitment on expiry — re-price a stale snipe, don't durable-nonce-replay it ([Helius congestion guide](https://www.helius.dev/blog/solana-congestion-how-to-best-send-solana-transactions), pattern reported). Policy rules: revert-protected Jito sends are exclusive (never raced with plain RPC); tips come from the tip_floor stream (target p75–p95 at launch), capped as a fraction (~50%) of modeled edge by the policy gate; rotate tip accounts on every provider. Fee estimation behind an interface: Helius getPriorityFeeEstimate backend when available, raw getRecentPrioritizationFees percentile fallback scoped to the bonding curve + global accounts (local fee markets make global levels irrelevant). Build per-lane landing telemetry (slot delta, land rate, cost) and auto-rank lanes from live data — no vendor benchmark covers launch-contention conditions.

## 3. Pump Protocol

Pump.fun's bonding-curve program (6EF8rrecthR5Dkzon8Nwu78hRvfCKubJ14M5uBEwF6P) moved to a unified v2 trade interface — buy_v2 (27 accounts), sell_v2 (26), buy_exact_quote_in_v2 — mandating volume accumulators, a sharing_config PDA, and separate fee_recipient + buyback_fee_recipient accounts; USDC-paired coins (live May 21, 2026) trade **only** via v2. Fees are dynamic market-cap tiers from a dedicated fee program (pfeeUxB6jkeY1Hxd7CsFCAjcbHA9rWtchMGdZ6VojVZ): bonding curve 1.25% total; PumpSwap 1.25% decaying to 0.30%. The last 12 months saw roughly one breaking change per quarter, including an **unannounced** Feb 2026 redeploy that broke every bot with a misleading Custom:6024 "overflow" error. CreateEvent and TradeEvent gained many fields — pre-2026 fixture-pinned decoders will not parse current logs.

## 5. Safety & Intel

The free safety stack is genuinely good: RugCheck's no-auth report endpoints are the best programmatic source, and 2025–26 academic work produced directly usable datasets and features. Key research findings: bundled-account supply share (~36.5% in risky tokens), early-buyer concentration, and short launchpad duration are most predictive of losses (MemeTrans); graduation base rates crashed 3.18x to 0.198% between late 2025 and mid-2026, and social links in metadata give an 8.9–17.4x graduation lift (RED-PUMP-2026); 1,012 persistent sniper-wallet rings were catalogued with addresses released (RED-COHORT-2026).

## 6. Open-Source Repos

Streaming/decoding is dominated by Yellowstone gRPC and the Rust ecosystem (0xfnzero, carbon); trading SDKs are healthiest in Rust but Pump.fun now publishes official TS SDKs, and Raydium/Meteora maintain first-party TS SDKs — directly reusable in an Electron/TS engine. On foundations, @solana/web3.js 2.x was renamed **@solana/kit** (v7.0.0, June 2026, Anza) and is the clear go-forward SDK. Nearly everything returned by "solana sniper bot" searches is keyword-stuffed bait or key-exfiltrating malware ([SlowMist analysis](https://slowmist.medium.com/threat-intelligence-an-analysis-of-a-malicious-solana-open-source-trading-bot-ab580fd3cc89)); flagged-pattern repos (hexnome/*, keidev-sol/*, cutupdev/*, hanshaze/*, machenxi/*, Takhi77/*) must not be installed or studied.
