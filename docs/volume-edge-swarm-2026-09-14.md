# Round-trip edge on the AMM leg — 2026-09-14

Six agents, one question: **is there a buy-setup-then-sell rule on liquid Solana tokens whose expected round-trip return clears the ~1.5 % cost floor plus impact?** Universe: graduated pump tokens on PumpSwap, study-grade (pool, day) cells from `tape/out/universe.csv`, nine day-files spanning 2026-07-25 → 09-14. Agents: **meanrev** (whale-dump reversion), **momentum** (buyer/inflow surges), **flow** (order-flow imbalance), **migdrift** (post-graduation drift), **smart** (follow repeat-profitable wallets), **session** (time-of-day). Every number below is **net** — after Krypt's fee, the pool fee, both impact legs, priority and base fees, ATA rent reclaimed, local builder route.

## The answer

**No.** Zero of six setups clears the floor out of sample, and five of the six are net-negative out of sample by 1 to 14 percentage points per round trip; the best out-of-sample cell produced anywhere in the swarm is **−0.41 %**, and the best cell chosen with full hindsight *on the test set itself* is **+0.01 %** (t = 0.01).

The more useful finding is *why*. Three of the six setups found a **real, replicating, correctly-signed** predictive signal — order-flow imbalance predicts the next 60 s (win rate rises monotonically 50.9 % → 87.4 % across deciles, September, n = 159,958); post-graduation drift is +3.1 to +4.0 % over the first five minutes at t = 2.9–5.6, six weeks apart; trades alongside repeat-profitable wallets beat matched ordinary trades by +0.62 pp in sample and +2.87 pp out. None of them is **big enough**. The signals are 0.3–3 % per round trip. The floor is 1.6 % fixed plus 0.7–1.8 % of impact. This is an arithmetic failure, not a prediction failure, and no threshold, horizon, size or hour fixes arithmetic.

## The cost floor, and whose money it is

| term | per round trip | source |
|---|---|---|
| Krypt fee, 0.50 %/side | **1.00 %** | `shared/fees.ts` `FEE_BPS = 50`, injected pre-signing, pinned by test |
| PumpSwap pool fee, measured VWAP 0.30 %/side (not the 0.25 % assumed in the brief) | **0.60 %** | 13.6 M swaps; `executedPriceSol` sits exactly ±30.0 bps from `quoteAmount/baseAmount` at the median |
| **fixed, size-independent subtotal** | **1.60 %** | |
| own impact, both legs, 100·S/L each | 0.55–1.80 % at 0.5 SOL | constant-product law validated held-out, slope 0.996 on 664 k independent-mid pairs |
| priority + 2 × 5,000-lamport sig | 0.60 % at 0.5 SOL (engine floor 0.00301 SOL) | `engine.ts` floors, not an auction price |
| **median realised, all-in** | **2.3 % – 3.4 %** | four agents independently: 3.42/3.34 (meanrev), 3.32/2.31 (momentum), 2.77/2.56 (session), 2.29 (flow) |

**Our own fee is 62.5 % of the fixed floor and 29–43 % of the all-in cost.** That has a sharp implication and it cuts both ways:

- **For the app's owner trading the treasury, the fee is a wash.** 0.80 % of the 1.00 % returns to the treasury; only the 0.20 % referral cut is real, and only on referred trades (`fee-and-referrals`). The owner's honest floor is therefore **0.60 % + impact + priority**, not 1.60 %.
- **For a user it is real, and it is most of the bar they have to clear.** A user needs a 1.6 % gross edge before impact to break even on a trade that moves nothing.
- **And the waiver does not save it.** The flow agent priced exactly this counterfactual: with Krypt's 1.00 % deleted the floor is **0.85 %** in the deepest depth decile, and the best replicating directional reading in September — 1 %-trimmed gross mean **+0.583 %** — still nets **−0.27 %**. The strongest signal we measured does not pay even when the house eats its own fee. That is the single most decisive number in this memo: our fee is not what is killing these strategies, it is only what makes them worse for the customer than for us.

One correction to the project cost table, measured this round: **on a freshly graduated pool the fee actually paid is ~1.20 %/side, not 0.30 %**. Measured as `|userQuoteAmount − quoteAmount|/quoteAmount` bucketed by pool age and replicated on an in-sample (07-28) and an out-of-sample (09-13) day: median buy 118.6 bps, median sell 120–125 bps in the first five minutes, decaying to 30 bps by 60–120 min. `lpFeeBps + protocolFeeBps` is only 25 bps, so ~95 bps is pump's coin-creator fee — highest exactly when a pool is new. In the first five minutes post-graduation the floor is **3.40 % before a basis point of impact**. `docs/strat-swarm-2026-07-24.md`'s table and the cost agent's 0.30 %/side term both understate that window by ~1.8 pp.

### meanrev — dead, and inverted

The headline was pre-registered on July before September existed: best in-sample net-mean cell with n > 1000 out of 188 sell-side configurations. It flipped sign, and the physics explains it without reference to any trade. Measuring `retrace(t) = (mid(t) − midPost)/(midPre − midPost)` on raw prints, the **median retrace at +60 s is 0.000 / 0.036 / 0.120 / 0.094** at own-impact thresholds of 2/5/10/20 % in sample, and **0.000 / 0.000 / −0.001 / −0.093** out of sample, decaying to −0.25 to −0.34 by +300 s. To pay the floor at θ = 10 % you need retrace ≥ 0.265; you get 0.120 in sample and −0.001 out. **An outsized sell's own impact on a graduated pump pool is 88–100 % permanent at one minute.** That is the one reusable measurement here, and it kills the entire family rather than merely pricing it out.

Everything else confirms it: 0 of 90 September single-print cells have a positive net mean *or* median; the July winner's top-5 pool-day share of net profit is 132 % against the project's ≤60 % bar; a BUY-side mirror is indistinguishable from an unconditional control (+0.54 % vs +0.55 % gross median), so there is no asymmetric bounce; deeper pools are *worse* (−51.91 % at minDepth ≥ 500 SOL, where the unconditional control is −1.28 %); and the out-of-sample **gross** mean is −10.42 % before a single fee. With zero costs it still loses.

### momentum — dead on statistics, with one cell left inconclusive

The pre-registered cell (variant C, Z = 4, ≥5 buyers, ≥3 SOL net inflow, H = 300 s) booked +0.52 % in sample on a 95 % CI of [−1.31, +2.53] — never significant — and −1.09 % out. Adding a **point-in-time** liquidity gate (the pool must already have 200 prints / 30 active minutes / 30 SOL depth *before* the signal, rather than over the whole day) moves in-sample to −0.001 %: part of the in-sample number was look-ahead, and the agent removed it rather than keeping it.

The sweep behaved unusually well, which makes the kill cleaner: Spearman ρ between in-sample and out-of-sample net over 120 (cell, horizon) pairs is **+0.859**. The surface is stable across a six-week regime gap and it sits at or below zero. The effect is a net-**inflow** effect, not a buyer-**count** effect (count variants show +0.04 to +0.17 pp lift over control and are the worst OOS cells at −3.6 to −4.0 %) — an independent reproduction of E14/G10 on a different leg of the market. It fully reverses by H = 900 s.

**Stated so it is not buried:** exactly one cell is net-positive in both samples — variant C, Z = 4, MINB = 5, MINF = 1 SOL, H = 300 s: +0.310 % (n = 1,061) in sample, +0.329 % (n = 451) out, +0.350 % pooled over nine day-files. Day-clustered CI [−0.419, +1.416], P(mean > 0) = 0.807, 6 of 9 day-files positive. It is indistinguishable from zero at day resolution and it is the runner-up to a pre-registered pick that lost money — the exact shape T7 warns about. **Recorded as inconclusive, not as an edge.**

### flow — the signal is real, replicates, and selects for rugs

This is the most informative failure. The directional signal is not in dispute: top-decile 60 s gross win rate 77.3 % (July) / 87.4 % (September), top-minus-bottom decile spread +23.0 / +32.3 pts, contemporaneous Spearman 0.73–0.96 confirming the decode. And it is **3.1–3.9× too small**: the best September readings are a top-decile median +0.284 %, trimmed mean +0.583 %, raw mean +0.114 %, against a 1.85 % cheapest-available round trip anywhere in graduated-pump PumpSwap.

Of 3,024 in-sample cells, 86 had a positive net mean, **zero** were positive on all three July days, and **zero** passed the concentration bar. The argmax cell booked +6.77 % in sample on 2 trades out of 5,839 carrying half its gross sum, a top-5-pool share of 390 %, a net median of −1.81 % and **8.2 % of trades net-positive**. Scored once on September: −2.01 %, t = −22.4, 0 of 1,000 bootstrap draws above zero. The zero-tuning literal version is −2.84 % on n = 120,915, t = −120.3.

The mechanism finding is new and worth keeping. The strongest raw signal in the sweep (R3) produces a September gross **median of +7.63 % at an 80.3 % win rate** — far above any floor — against a gross **mean of −1.70 %**. Winners average +9.72 %; the 19.7 % of losers average −48.34 %, 5th percentile −99.97 %. P(position loses > 90 % within the 5-minute hold) rises **monotonically with the signal**: 0.90 % in decile 2 → 7.41 % in decile 10. The worst trade (pool `FfZsnPbk…`, 09-11 06:12 UTC) is six consecutive minutes of textbook accumulation into a growing 2,858 SOL pool, then one minute of 1,590 sells and 2,912 SOL draining it — mid 4.86e-1 → 2.87e-5. **Sustained one-sided buy pressure in a deep graduated pool is, a measurable fraction of the time, the operator building the book it is about to sell into.** The feature that predicts continuation is the feature that predicts the drain, and in September the drain wins outright: the gross mean is negative in 9 of 10 deciles before any cost.

### migdrift — the direction is true, the trade is not

Do not file this as "no post-migration drift". Gross mid-to-mid drift from +800 ms is **+1.80 % @30 s / +2.40 % @60 s / +3.17 % @3 min in July (t = 2.1–2.5)** and **+3.31 % / +3.12 % / +3.51 % in September (t = 2.9–5.6)**, up in 56–80 % of cases, on all 5,367 migrations with no activity filter. It then inverts hard: −19.1 %/−12.2 % at 10 min, −32.2 %/−32.9 % at 60 min.

It dies five over-determined ways: the pre-registered carry-forward is −0.41 %; September's own best cell chosen with full hindsight is **+0.01 %**; every entry later than 800 ms is worse (+30 s → −2.99 %, t = −2.95); it is negative at every size in both samples (−5.96 % at 5 SOL); and 3 of 5 days are negative in each sample with every positive day breaching the concentration bar. The proximate cause is the ~1.20 %/side fresh-pool fee above — but **it still fails if you delete that fee**: re-run at a counterfactual 30 bps/side, July nets +0.02 / +0.14 / −0.48 % at 2/3/5 min (t ≤ 0.09) and September +0.89 % at 5 min (t = 0.63). The verdict does not depend on the fee dispute.

### smart — the skill is real; it is smaller than our fee

Two things are true at once and neither is the hypothesis. **The skill is real:** the July cohort (1,836 wallets, +14,845 SOL over two mining days) made +913 SOL realized on held-out 07-27 while the entire 248,781-wallet population lost −150,705 SOL; day-to-day persistence lift is 1.29× (July) and 9.4× (September) over base. **The follow signal replicates in relative terms:** pool × hour matched advantage over an ordinary buyer is +0.62 pp in sample and +2.87 pp out, positive on all five test days at every horizon.

And it is arithmetically uncopyable. On 07-27 the cohort earned +913.17 SOL on 269,225 SOL of volume over 228,621 round trips = **+0.34 % per round trip**. A follower pays 1.60 % in fees alone. Every arm, horizon, ticket size (0.1/0.25/0.5/1/2 SOL and the analytic cost-minimising size) and admissible day is negative; the best honest cell anywhere is −0.32 %. Selecting on the *top* performers inverts (September top-50 made −19,060 SOL on day 2), reproducing W4 with persistent ids — the ones that did not exist when that finding was first made. The list rots: 15.3 % of the July cohort still active six weeks later, and those 280 wallets lost −296.5 SOL.

Two footnotes that matter more than the verdict. **The shipped `isSmart` flag cannot be tested at all in this universe**: across 114,404,291 lines there are 516 `isSmart:true` rows, all carrying one address — the hard-coded seed in `electron/engine/watchlist.ts:15` — which appears **zero times** in 33.9 M mined AMM swaps. It is a bookmark, not a measurement. And **latency is what would have faked this**: re-running with a 0 ms fill instead of 800 ms turns the 300 s cell from −0.461 % to **+0.288 %**. T3 reproduced exactly; the honest-fill discipline is the whole difference between publishable and dead.

### session — time-of-day cannot pay for a round trip

Pre-registered: take the top-6 July hours by net edge, run them unchanged on September. In sample +3.714 % (n = 5,184); those same hours in September **−3.071 %** (n = 3,522). Textbook T7, and this time a new universe for a family already killed twice (R1/R2).

The setup-independent version is the part to keep. Hour-to-hour variation in realised round-trip cost across both samples is **0.439 pp**; in 0.5 SOL impact, **0.258 pp**; total cost variation any hour-selection rule can address, **0.697 pp**. Against an hour-invariant fixed floor of **2.202 pp** and an out-of-sample gap to break-even of 3.645 pp. Trading only the single cheapest hour of the day closes less than a third of the gap. Re-costing every trade at its own cost-minimising size — the most generous cost any size can produce — moves September from −3.645 % to −3.370 % and leaves 0 of 24 hours positive. What *does* persist is activity (ρ = 0.676 on swaps per pool-minute); returns (ρ = +0.25) and costs (ρ = +0.02) do not.

## What would have to be true

Falsifiable conditions, each with the measured value against it:

1. **A gross round-trip edge ≥ 2.3–3.4 % that survives out of sample.** Best measured: top-decile 60 s median +0.284 %, trimmed mean +0.583 %; leaders' own realised edge +0.34 %/trip; post-graduation drift +3.1–4.0 % in a window whose own fee is 2.40 %. *Falsified at every reading.*
2. **Or: the house fee waived is enough.** Floor becomes 0.85 % in the deepest decile; the best replicating signal nets **−0.27 %**. *Falsified — measured directly.*
3. **Or: a venue whose all-in floor is ≤ ~1.0 %.** Cheapest round trip available anywhere in this universe is 1.85 %, at 0.5 SOL into a ~5,000 SOL pool. *Not falsified — not testable here. There is no blue-chip book on this tape.* This is the only open lever.
4. **Or: sub-slot execution.** A 0 ms fill flips smart-money 300 s to +0.288 % and inflates flow's in-sample by 5.6 pp. Prior art already measured the on-curve version of this: break-even exit latency 150–250 ms, inside one Solana slot (E3). *Falsified as reachable.*
5. **Mean-reversion requires retrace ≥ cost/impact** (0.265 at θ = 10 %, 0.138 at θ = 20 %). Measured 0.120 / 0.094 in sample, −0.001 / −0.093 out. *Falsified, and the sign is wrong.*
6. **Hour/regime selection requires the cost channel to vary by more than the floor**, or the return channel's profile to persist. 0.697 pp of addressable variation vs 2.202 pp of floor; ρ(net, July vs Sept) = +0.25. *Falsified both ways.*
7. **The tail must be survivable.** R2 has 8.2 % of trades net-positive and a −48 % mean loser; R3's 5th percentile is −99.97 %. A stop is the untested variant, and X4 says −35 % stops fill at 0.44–0.53× in this asset class, i.e. a stop realises most of that loss anyway. *Not falsified, but the prior is bad and I did not measure it.*
8. **For the inconclusive momentum cell:** sd is ~29 % per trade against a ~0.35 % claimed mean, so separating it from zero at day-cluster resolution needs **10,000–13,000 trades ≈ 70–90 trading days** at the observed ~150 fills/day. By the TESTABILITY DECLARATION that is on the testable side of the line — but it is a three-month forward test, not something this tape can answer.

## What to measure next, cheapest decisive first

1. **Read `migShadow`'s forward records.** Cost: zero. The instrument has been on by default since 2026-07-27, three lanes (`block0` / `react400` / `react800`), honest constant-product fills, 5 % cap — and **no document has ever evaluated its output**. The migration block is the only family in the corpus with a measured, day-robust, *reachable* positive row (mid-lead position-1 +4.6/+4.6/+6.5 %; capped position-2 +0.11–0.34 per fill). The kill condition is already written: if `react400` does not fill at position ≤ 2 with positive P&L for a sustained stretch, close the family. Nothing in this swarm should be run before this is read.
2. **Rebuild the cost table once, with three columns the corpus does not have**: (a) Krypt's 1.00 %/round trip, which post-dates the 07-24 table; (b) the fee-waived column, since that is the only term the owner controls and this memo shows it is not enough; (c) the measured fresh-pool 1.20 %/side creator fee and its age decay. Arithmetic only, no new data. Every verdict in `docs/` is denominated in a floor that is now wrong in both directions.
3. **Price the priority-tip auction** (M9 — "the unmeasured number"). Edge at migration position 1 is 0.217 SOL on a 5 SOL trade; if landing top-2 costs more than ~0.15 SOL in tips it is gone. This gates item 1's positive rows and nothing else in the corpus can be decided without it. Incumbents' median largest first-5s buy went 12 → 871 SOL between 07-25 and 07-26, so the prior is poor.
4. **Extend the universe to a real large-cap book** — the 38,297 pools that graduated before 2026-07-25, and Raydium/Orca. Cost: an inverted pump historical mint list or ~38 k `getAccountInfo` calls, plus a decoder per venue. This is the **only** condition above (#3) that is not already falsified, and all six agents independently named its absence as the binding limit. Expect it to move the floor from 1.85 % toward ~1.6 % + small impact — which is still above every signal measured here, so run it to close the question, not because it is likely to pay.
5. **Paper-forward the one inconclusive momentum cell**, pre-registered and frozen: variant C, Z = 4, MINB = 5, MINF = 1 SOL, H = 300 s, 0.5 SOL, point-in-time gate. ~150 fills/day, 70–90 days, zero cost in paper. Declare it a three-month test before starting it, per T18.
6. **Do not run another sweep.** 3,024 + 188 + 120 cells were swept here on top of ~4,000 + 7,500 + 4,860 + 2,304 + 2,887 + 120 + 46 already in the registry, with the same result. `tape-audit-2026-07-25.md` §Recommendation item 1 already says "No strategy swarm", and `farming-swarm-2026-08-15.md:836` already diagnoses why: *the binding constraint is the search process, and changing the asset class does not touch it.* This swarm changed the asset class (the AMM leg, S4's #1 unanswered infra item) and re-derived the same pathologies — T7 in five of six agents, T8 in four, T13 in four, T3 in two. That is another data point for the diagnosis, not against it.

## What this research could NOT establish

- **The question as asked, on "liquid Solana tokens", is untestable on this tape.** There is no SOL/BONK/WIF/JUP, no Raydium, no Orca. "Deep" here means 85–5,000 SOL of PumpSwap quote reserve on a graduated pump token. Every agent tested the deepest thing that exists in the data and none relabelled it as a blue chip. A real large-cap book could have a materially lower floor and was not measured.
- **38,297 SOL-plausible pools that graduated before 2026-07-25** cannot be named from this tape — likely the oldest and deepest, exactly where a continuation effect would have its best shot against the floor. Not proxied.
- **The shipped `isSmart` flag has n = 0 in this universe.** Its one hard-coded address never trades on the AMM leg. Its curve-side behaviour is already on record (7-second median holds, 2–4 s detect-and-land, arithmetically uncopyable) and was not re-derived.
- **Coverage is thin and day-files are not days.** Only 07-27 (100 %) and 09-11 (99.4 %) span 24 h; 07-25 is 18.6 h, 07-26 21.5 h, 07-28 6.9 h, 09-10 8.7 h, 09-12 14 h, 09-13 ~5 h usable. Roughly **2.5 complete-day-equivalents in sample and ~2.0 out**. 09-14 was excluded: the recorder is still appending to it and the payload count exceeds the inventory's by 871,860 rows.
- **Survivorship is present and unremoved at the pool-day level.** The study-grade gate (≥200 swaps, ≥30 active minutes across the whole day) conditions on activity that occurs after entries, favouring pools that stayed alive. It biases returns **up**, so it cannot have produced a negative verdict — but it means true numbers are worse, and the rug frequencies in flow's finding 4 are understated. The momentum agent quantified the cost of removing the intra-day part: +0.518 % → −0.001 %.
- **Priority-fee realism is the engine's floor (0.00301 SOL/round trip), not an auction price.** Every surge, flag and migration these rules trade into is contested by construction. Real landing costs more; every number above is optimistic by that amount.
- **The relayer route is not costed.** All numbers assume the local builder (default since 09-01). Relayer adds 1.00 %/round trip and makes everything ~2 pp worse.
- **Shorting is not implementable on these pools**, so negative long-short spreads are descriptive only.
- **A stop-loss overlay on flow's R3 is the one variant not tested.** X4's measured gap ratios say a stop would realise most of the −48 % mean loser anyway, but I did not measure it and will not claim it.
- **Feed loss:** the `feed_health` rows (12.0–21.6 % mean, max 44.5 %) measure the **bonding-curve** feed and do not describe the AMM tape these studies run on. Measured directly on the exact windows used, by exact 64-bit reserve continuity: **99.79 %–99.997 % contiguous**, i.e. 0.003–0.208 % loss, worst on 09-12. Loss cannot manufacture a return here — every mark is an actual on-chain pool state, so a dropped event can only cause a missed trigger. Separately, `feedHealth.ts`'s slot-ordering fix is dated **today**, so every curve-feed figure in the corpus is an upper bound of unknown tightness.
- **None of the six results was adversarially verified** — all six were dead on arrival and the verifier pass was not spent. Each verdict rests on its own agent's numbers. The mitigating fact is that every disclosed bias (day-level survivorship, excluded unresolved trades, engine-floor priority fees, the generous derived mid, the relayer omission) runs **in favour of the candidate**, so the negatives are safe and the one inconclusive cell is the one to distrust.
- **Capacity was never the binding constraint and was not stressed.** The median study-grade pool supports ~2 SOL at 1 % impact against a median actual trade of 0.026–0.098 SOL. Above ~2–5 SOL every rule trades against its own impact. These strategies die on statistics well before they die on ceiling — which is another way of saying a bigger book would not have helped.
